# Commit Hike

Every commit you make moves you along a hiking trail, right inside VS Code.
A day of steady work takes you about ten kilometers along a real mountain
ridge, a desert crossing or a folk tale.

- **Real trails.** The Chornohora ridge over Hoverla and the Tongariro Alpine Crossing on their real GPS tracks, plus original stories.
- **A hike you can watch.** A side-on scene that follows the route's real elevation, the weather and your clock. Scroll back along the way you came.
- **A real map.** A topographic map of the whole trail: zoom in like on paper, the scale bar follows and more names appear.
- **Stories, facts and achievements** at every stop, streaks and your daily distance.
- **Your own routes.** Create one from a template and import it as a folder or a `.zip`.
- **Walk together.** Everyone committing to a project on the same trail, with a leaderboard.
- **English and Ukrainian.**

## Private by design

- **Nothing touches your projects.** No git hooks, no files in your repositories: Commit Hike only reads your git history.
- **Nothing leaves your computer.** No accounts, no network requests, no telemetry.
- **Real work counts.** Lock files, generated code, whitespace-only changes and other people's commits don't.

## Getting started

Open the **Commit Hike** view in the activity bar. A short setup asks which
projects count and where your journey starts. Then just commit.

All commands are in the command palette under *Commit Hike*: choosing a trail,
importing your own route, the hiker icon, difficulty, language and more.
